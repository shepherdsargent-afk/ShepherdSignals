Strategy
Opens with a specific, believable accusation — not a generic pitch. "Did you notice?" creates a pattern interrupt. GMs know their costs go up; they rarely know by exactly how much. The subject line will get opens because it feels personal and timely.
Why it works
No fluff. No "I hope this finds you well." Direct, credible, and specific. The $38K–$62K range is real enough to be credible, specific enough to create urgency. The free audit CTA has zero risk — the barrier is a month of invoices, not a credit card.
Call to action
Soft reply CTA — "Worth a look?" The lowest possible friction. No calendar link, no form. A one-word reply is enough to qualify the lead and trigger Shepherd's callback workflow.
Personalisation tips
For best results: research the club's known supplier (often listed on their website or LinkedIn). Replace [SUPPLIER] with the real name. Mention the specific city or province. Clubs in BC can reference BCLDB pricing — very specific and credible.

Why breakup emails work
Counterintuitively, the final email in a sequence often gets the highest reply rate. When prospects know contact is ending, many who were passively interested finally respond. "Is this the last one?" triggers a different psychological response than the previous 5 emails. Typically 20–35% of all replies in a sequence come from the breakup email.
Re-engagement
Add non-responders to a 90-day re-engagement sequence. Golf clubs think in seasons — a GM who ignores emails in January may respond in March when their budget cycle starts, or in October when contracts renew. Never delete a warm prospect; just cool them down.
